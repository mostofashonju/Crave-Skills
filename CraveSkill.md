## 4. Market and Price Intelligence

The foundation of every CraveSkill price is a real, recent wholesale market price from the user's mentioned country and city.

### 4.1 The Golden Rule

> Never guess a price. Every price CraveSkill uses must be dated, located, and sourced. Assumptions are always labeled as assumptions.

### 4.2 Price Sourcing Priority

| Priority | Source | Trust Level | How It Is Marked |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | User's own supplier bills and invoices | Highest | Verified — user data |
| 2 | Recent published wholesale rates for the named market — for example Bangladesh: Department of Agricultural Marketing (DAM) daily reports, city corporation daily price lists, TCB (Trading Corporation of Bangladesh), national daily market pages | High | Verified — source name and date |
| 3 | Recent web search of the named market and city | Medium | Searched — date and link noted |
| 4 | CraveSkill fallback estimate | Lowest | Assumption — must be replaced by real prices |

### 4.3 Default Wholesale Market Map — Bangladesh

When the user mentions Bangladesh without more detail, CraveSkill defaults to Dhaka and this market map.

| Market | City | Best For |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Chawkbazar | Old Dhaka | Wholesale dry goods — rice, lentils, sugar, spices, oil, dates (Ramadan hub) |
| Town Hall Market | Dhaka | Restaurant supplies — sauces, mayonnaise, cheese, condiments, packaging |
| Khatunganj | Chattogram | Edible oil, lentils, spices — the wholesale backbone of the port city |
| Local kitchen market | Any city | Fallback for small-volume perishables |

For any other country or city, CraveSkill first asks: which city and which wholesale market do you buy from — then builds a market map for that location.

### 5.1 Skill 1 — Food Costing

**Purpose:** Convert raw wholesale prices into the exact cost of one plated portion.

**Core formulas:**

- Edible portion quantity = As-purchased quantity × Yield percentage
- Cost per usable kilogram = As-purchased price per kilogram ÷ Yield percentage
- Portion food cost = Sum of every ingredient's edible portion cost in the portion
- Food cost percentage = Portion food cost ÷ Selling price × 100

**Typical food cost percentage targets (guidelines — tune to your concept):**

| Concept | Typical Food Cost % |
| --- | --- |
| Cloud kitchen / delivery only | 25 – 32% |
| Fast food and quick service | 27 – 35% |
| Café with strong beverage mix | 28 – 35% overall (beverages often 15 – 25%) |
| Casual dining / full service | 30 – 35% |
| Bakery and desserts | 30 – 38% |

**How CraveSkill applies it:**

1. Collect dated wholesale prices for the user's city and market
2. Convert as-purchased prices to usable (edible portion) costs using yield factors
3. Build the recipe costing sheet per portion and per batch (Template T2)
4. Compute food cost percentage at current and proposed selling prices
5. Flag any item outside the target band

**Pitfalls CraveSkill guards against:** ignoring trim and cooking loss, costing at retail instead of wholesale prices, forgetting oil-gas-seasoning allowance, and letting portions drift without a scale.

### 5.2 Skill 2 — Contribution Margin

**Purpose:** Measure how much money each dish leaves after variable costs — the true engine of profit.

**Core formulas:**

- Contribution margin per item = Selling price − All variable costs of that item
- Contribution margin ratio = Contribution margin ÷ Selling price × 100
- Contribution per bottleneck minute = Contribution margin ÷ Minutes of the busiest resource used (grill, burner, fryer, prep table)

**Decision rules:**

| Situation | Reading | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High price, high food cost %, high contribution | Volume-price item | Keep, monitor cost weekly |
| Low contribution, high popularity | Traffic builder | Raise price slowly or cut portion cost |
| Low contribution, low popularity | Candidate for removal | Drop or restructure |
| Highest contribution per bottleneck minute | Kitchen's best use of time | Push during rush hours |

**Key insight:** Food cost percentage alone can mislead. A 40% food cost item with a large contribution can be more profitable than a 25% food cost item with a tiny one. CraveSkill always reports both.

### 5.3 Skill 3 — Menu Engineering

**Purpose:** Combine popularity and profitability to classify every menu item and prescribe one action per item.

**Classification thresholds:**

- Popularity benchmark = (100% ÷ Number of items in the category) × 0.70 — the classic Kasavana–Smith rule
- Profitability benchmark = Category average contribution margin

**The matrix:**

| Class | Popularity | Contribution | Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ⭐ Star | High | High | Protect quality, keep price, feature prominently, never discount deeply |
| 🐴 Plowhorse | High | Low | Raise price in small steps, reduce portion cost, or rework the recipe |
| 🧩 Puzzle | Low | High | Rename, relocate, photograph, describe better, sample, or bundle with a star |
| 🐶 Dog | Low | Low | Remove, replace, or convert to a limited-time offer |

**Cadence:** Run the matrix monthly using 4 weeks of sales data (Template T4).

### 5.4 Skill 4 — Break-even Analysis

**Purpose:** Know exactly how many covers or orders are needed to cover fixed costs every month.

**Core formulas:**

- Contribution margin per unit = Selling price − Variable cost per unit
- Break-even units = Total fixed costs ÷ Contribution margin per unit
- Break-even sales value = Total fixed costs ÷ Contribution margin ratio
- Break-even per day = Break-even units ÷ Operating days

**Typical fixed cost blocks:** rent, salaries, utilities, internet and software, license renewals, marketing retainer, depreciation.

**How CraveSkill applies it:**

1. List all monthly fixed costs from the user's books
2. Compute blended contribution margin from the sales mix
3. Produce break-even units per month, per week, and per day
4. Compare with current volume to show the profit gap or safety cushion
5. Show which lever moves break-even fastest: price, food cost, or fixed cost

### 5.5 Skill 5 — Unit Economics

**Purpose:** Judge every delivery order on its own bottom line, platform by platform.

**The Unit Economics Equation — CraveSkill's law for every order:**

> Selling Price − Food Cost − Packaging − Platform Commission − Discounts − Other Variable Costs = Contribution

**Worked skeleton (fill with live numbers):**

| Element | Symbol | Your Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Selling price (platform menu price) | P | [P] |
| Food cost | FC | [FC] |
| Packaging | PK | [PK] |
| Platform commission (percent of P) | c × P | [c] |
| Discounts (platform and seller funded, percent of P) | d × P | [d] |
| Other variable costs (percent of P) | v × P | [v] |
| **Contribution** | **C** | **[C]** |

**Platform price solver** — set the platform price backward from a target contribution:

> Platform price = (Food cost + Packaging + Other variable fixed costs + Target contribution) ÷ (1 − Commission % − Discount % − Other variable %)

**Health checks:**

| Metric | Healthy Guideline |
| --- | --- |
| Net contribution rate (Contribution ÷ P) | 20% or more on platforms |
| Contribution per order | Must exceed average fixed cost per order |
| Discount depth | Rarely above 15% without a volume justification |

### 5.6 Skill 6 — Pricing Strategy

**Purpose:** Know when to raise, lower, bundle, or restructure prices — with rules, not feelings.

| Signal | Correct Action | Watch-outs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Item is a Star, demand steady, costs rising | Raise price 3 – 5% | Do it quietly; keep below the next psychological threshold |
| Demand drops sharply after a small increase | Elastic demand — lower back or restructure | Test with bundles instead of straight cuts |
| Puzzle item with high margin | Bundle with a Star (combo) | Do not discount the Star itself |
| Platform net contribution is negative | Restructure: platform-only price, portion change, or packaging change | Never sell at a loss for ranking alone |
| Off-peak hours are dead | Time-based offers at marginal prices | Floor price = variable cost only, and only for idle capacity |
| Competitor undercutting on commodity items (fries, drinks) | Do not chase — differentiate with signature items | Compete on value, not on commodity price |
| New item launch | Introductory price for 2 weeks, then target price | Announce the ending date |
| Costs spiked temporarily (onions, oil) | Short-term recipe or portion engineering | Avoid whiplash menu price changes |

**Psychological pricing techniques:**

- Charm endings: 249 and 299 read far below 250 and 300
- Anchor: place one premium-priced signature item first to make the rest feel reasonable
- Remove currency symbols where the design allows — clean price tags spend higher
- Good–Better–Best: three sizes steer most buyers to the middle
- Decoy: an unattractive large size pushes buyers toward the profitable regular size
- Never end a price list in round stacked numbers — break the pattern so items are compared on value, not price

### 5.7 Skill 7 — Inventory and Waste Control

**Purpose:** Small leakages destroy food businesses — find and plug every one.

**Core formulas:**

- Reorder point = (Average daily usage × Lead time in days) + Safety stock
- Safety stock = 1.65 × Demand standard deviation × Square root of lead time days (for ~95% service level)
- Inventory variance % = (Book stock − Physical stock) ÷ Book stock × 100 — target within 2%
- Waste % = Value of waste ÷ Value of purchases × 100 — lean target ≤ 2%, acceptable ≤ 5%

**Leakage map:**

| Leak | Typical Loss | Countermeasure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Over-prepping for slow days | High | Forecast-based prep par by daypart |
| Trim and cutting losses | Medium | Yield-tested cutting specs, staff training |
| Portion drift (heavy hands) | High daily | Scales, ladles, portion cups, patty molds |
| Spoilage and expiry | Medium | FIFO/FEFO labels, weekly counts, buy-to-par |
| Unlogged waste and staff meals | Medium | Waste log, staff meal policy with limits |
| Delivery errors and refunds | Low but deadly for ratings | Order verification checklist before handoff |

Full systems live in Section 12 (inventory) and Section 13 (waste).

### 5.8 Skill 8 — Sales and Customer Analytics

**Purpose:** Track what customers buy, when they buy, and whether they return — then act on it.

**Core metrics:**

| Metric | Formula | Why It Matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Average order value (AOV) | Revenue ÷ Orders | The cheapest growth lever |
| Orders per day / by daypart | Orders ÷ Days | Staffing and prep planning |
| Item mix % | Item sales ÷ Total sales | Feeds menu engineering |
| Attach rate | Add-on sales ÷ Orders | Drinks and desserts lift profit fast |
| Repeat rate | Returning customers ÷ Total customers | Delivery businesses live or die here |
| Cohort retention | % of a month's customers ordering again in month 1, 2, 3 | Spots quality decay early |
| RFM score | Recency, Frequency, Monetary per customer | Targets offers precisely |

**Method set:** Pareto/ABC analysis (typically ~20% of items drive ~80% of sales), day-of-week and hour heat tables, basket analysis (what is bought together → combos), moving averages and trend lines, seasonality calendar (paydays, Fridays, Ramadan, exams, winter).

**Action loop:** measure weekly → classify items → adjust prep and promotion → measure again.

### 5.9 Skill 9 — Delivery Platform Analytics

**Purpose:** For Foodpanda, foodi, khaodao, Pathao Food, and others — analyze commission, platform fees, promotions, discounts, ranking, conversion, and net contribution.

**Effective commission rate:**

> Effective commission % = (Commission + Retained platform fees + Seller-funded promo share + Payment fees) ÷ Gross order value × 100

The headline commission is never the whole story — effective commission is usually several points higher.

**Platform scorecard (per platform, updated monthly):**

| Metric | This Platform | Best Platform | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Effective commission % | [%] | [%] | [Keep / Watch / Exit] |
| Orders per month | [n] | [n] | — |
| Net contribution per order | [value] | [value] | — |
| Net contribution total | [value] | [value] | — |
| Conversion (orders ÷ menu views) | [%] | [%] | Fix menu or photos |
| Average rating | [x.x] | [x.x] | Below 4.2 is urgent |
| Prep time / rider wait | [min] | [min] | Ranking lever |
| Cancel and refund rate | [%] | [%] | Ranking lever |

**Ranking levers CraveSkill works on:** menu photo quality, keyword-rich item titles, review velocity and responses, conversion rate, prep time, order acceptance rate, uptime/availability hours, cancellation rate.

**Promotion ROI:**

> Promotion ROI = (Incremental orders × Contribution per order − Promotion cost) ÷ Promotion cost

A promo that adds orders but destroys net contribution is a loss dressed as marketing — CraveSkill shows the math before you hit Accept.

**Multi-platform rules:**

- Price each platform separately using the platform price solver (Section 5.5) — never copy dine-in prices onto a 30% commission platform
- Compare platforms on net contribution total, not on order count
- Build a direct channel (WhatsApp, phone, own delivery rider) to escape commission on repeat customers — reward direct orders with a small loyalty perk

### 5.10 Skill 10 — Cash Flow and Profitability

**Purpose:** A restaurant can have high sales but poor cash flow and little or no profit — CraveSkill keeps the two separate and tracks both.

**Core formulas:**

- Prime cost = Cost of goods sold + Total labor cost — target ≤ 60 – 65% of sales
- Net profit margin = (Revenue − All costs) ÷ Revenue × 100 — healthy band 5 – 15%
- Daily cash position = Cash in − (Purchases + Labor paid + Expenses + Owner drawings)
- Cash conversion gap = Days of inventory + Days of receivables − Days of payables

**Cash flow calendar for delivery businesses:**

| Flow | Timing | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Walk-in sales cash | Daily | Covers daily perishable purchases |
| Platform payouts | Weekly or biweekly | Do not fund daily buying from a weekly payout — ring-fence it |
| Wholesaler credit | Often 7 – 15 days for known customers | Negotiate credit aligned to your payout cycle |
| Rent, salaries, utilities | Fixed monthly dates | Reserve cash from daily sales weekly — never spend it |

**Weekly cash discipline:** daily cash sheet (Template T10), weekly stock-to-purchase reconciliation, monthly profit and loss with prime cost check, and a 13-week rolling cash forecast.

## 6. Quantity and Ratio Analysis for Pricing

CraveSkill prices from physical quantities first, money second. Every price is anchored to what actually goes in the plate.

### 6.1 From Purchase to Plate — The Quantity Chain

> As-purchased → trim yield → edible portion → cooking yield → cooked weight → portion size → portions per batch

### 6.2 Typical Yield Factors (verify with your own kitchen yield tests)

| Ingredient | Typical Edible Yield | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Potato (peeled) | 82 – 88% | Peel loss 12 – 18% |
| Onion (peeled) | 88 – 92% | Trim loss 8 – 12% |
| Tomato (cored) | 90 – 95% | — |
| Carrot (scrapped) | 82 – 88% | — |
| Whole chicken → boneless | 60 – 70% | Bones and skin reduce yield |
| Beef quarter → boneless | 55 – 65% | Bone-in is cheaper per kilogram but heavier |
| Fish whole → fillet | 35 – 55% | Species dependent |

### 6.3 Typical Cooking Yields

| Item | Raw → Cooked | Rule of Thumb |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rice | × 2.2 – 2.5 weight | 1 kg raw serves 10 – 12 large portions |
| Chicken | − 25 – 30% weight | 110 g raw patty ≈ 80 g cooked |
| Beef patty | − 25 – 30% weight | 100 g raw ≈ 75 g cooked |
| Deep-fried items | − 10 – 20% weight | Moisture out, some oil in |
| Pasta | × 2.2 – 2.6 weight | 90 g dry ≈ 220 g cooked |

### 6.4 Core Ratio Table (commercial standards)

| Dish / Preparation | Ratio | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plain rice : water | 1 : 1.75 – 2 | By volume |
| Polao rice : water | 1 : 1.25 | Aged rice needs slightly more |
| Kacchi biryani rice : meat | 1 kg : 1 kg | Classic kacchi balance |
| Kacchi biryani rice : water | 1 : 1 – 1.1 | Absorbed with the meat stock |
| Pizza dough hydration | Flour : Water = 100 : 58 – 65 | Baker's percentage |
| Burger patty raw weight | 100 – 130 g | Shrinks to 75 – 95 g cooked |
| Shawarma chicken per wrap | 80 – 100 g | Plus 15 – 20 g sauce |
| Pound cake | Butter : Sugar : Egg : Flour = 1 : 1 : 1 : 1 | By weight |
| Tea (leaf per liter) | 10 – 12 g | Milk tea, strong brew |
| Coffee (ground per liter) | 55 – 60 g | Standard brew ratio 1 : 16 – 1 : 18 |
| Mayonnaise base | 1 yolk : 200 – 240 ml oil | House mayo beats bought on cost |
| Simple syrup | Sugar : Water = 1 : 1 | By weight |

### 6.5 Quantity-to-Price Method

1. Fix the portion spec in grams for every ingredient (Template T1)
2. Convert to edible portion need, then to as-purchased need using yield factors
3. Multiply by dated wholesale prices → portion food cost
4. Apply the target food cost percentage → floor price
5. Check competitor prices and psychological thresholds → final price
6. Lock the spec — any portion change is repriced through this chain again

**Example of usable-cost math:** Potato at 40 per kilogram with 85% peel yield → usable cost = 40 ÷ 0.85 = 47.1 per kilogram. A 150 g fry portion costs 7.1 in potato alone before oil, coating, and gas.

## 9. Statistical Strategies — Least Cost Combination and Profit Maximization

### 9.1 Least Cost Combination Strategy

**Definition:** Achieve a required output at a required quality standard at the minimum total cost by choosing the optimal mix of inputs — ingredient blends, suppliers, package sizes, and portions.

**Where it applies:**

| Problem | Least-Cost Question |
| --- | --- |
| Rice for biryani | What blend of premium and standard rice hits the aroma and texture standard at lowest cost per kilogram? |
| Burger patties | What mix of beef chuck and brisket (or chicken thigh share) hits the flavor-fat target at least cost? |
| Cheese for pizza | What blend of mozzarella types gives the melt-stretch standard at least cost? |
| Suppliers | Which market or supplier basket meets the full ingredient list at minimum landed cost? |
| Order sizes | Bulk discount vs spoilage and carrying cost — what order quantity is cheapest overall? |
| Value meals | What protein-carb-vegetable basket meets the calorie and presentation standard at least cost? |

**Six-step method:**

1. Define the objective — minimize total cost of the mix
2. List the decision variables — quantities of each input
3. Write the constraints — quality standard, recipe spec, demand, storage, shelf life, budget
4. Gather dated prices for every input
5. Solve — with two variables, compare corner points of the feasible region in a table; with more, use systematic trial mixes in a spreadsheet
6. Verify — cook a test batch; quality is a hard constraint, not a nice-to-have

**Worked micro-example — rice blend (illustrative prices, replace with dated market prices):**

Standard: aroma blend must contain at least 30% premium rice. Weekly need: 100 kg.

| Mix | Premium @ 130/kg | Standard @ 85/kg | Quality Check | Total Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Current practice 50 : 50 | 50 kg = 6,500 | 50 kg = 4,250 | Pass | 10,750 |
| Least cost 30 : 70 | 30 kg = 3,900 | 70 kg = 5,950 | Pass (exactly 30%) | 9,850 |
| Below standard 20 : 80 | 20 kg = 2,600 | 80 kg = 6,800 | **Fail** | Rejected |

Saving: 900 per week (8.4%) with quality held at the constraint — same logic scales to every blend in the kitchen.

**Substitution matrix habit:** keep a standing table of acceptable substitutes per key ingredient (oil types, rice grades, cheese types, packaging sizes) with quality limits and price deltas — when a price spikes, the substitution decision is already half-made.

### 9.2 Profit Maximization Strategy

**Definition:** Allocate limited kitchen capacity, marketing spend, and menu attention to the items and hours that generate the highest total contribution.

**Core formulas:**

- Total contribution = Σ (Contribution per item × Units sold per item)
- Contribution per bottleneck minute = Contribution ÷ Minutes on the busiest resource
- Price elasticity of demand = % change in quantity ÷ % change in price
- Capacity utilization = Actual productive hours ÷ Available hours

**Decision rules:**

| Finding | Profit-Maximizing Move |
| --- | --- |
| Item A has double the contribution per grill-minute of item B | Push A in rush hours; move B to off-peak or to pre-prep |
| Demand is inelastic (elasticity below 1) | Raising price raises revenue — test +3 – 5% |
| Demand is elastic (elasticity above 1) | Price cuts must be paid back by volume — verify with contribution math, never with hope |
| Idle capacity in dead hours | Sell at marginal prices (floor = variable cost) — a 20%-contribution off-peak combo beats an empty fryer |
| Marketing spend | Fund only channels where incremental contribution exceeds spend (promotion ROI > 0) |
| Menu mix drifting to low-margin items | Re-engineer layout, bundles, and server/platform suggestions to steer mix upward |

**Elasticity testing ladder:** change one price at a time → hold 2 weeks → measure volume change → compute elasticity → keep, revert, or adjust → record the result in the pricing log. Never test two changes at once.

### 9.3 Statistical Toolkit

| Tool | Formula / Method | CraveSkill Use Case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mean, median, mode | Averages and typical values | Typical Tuesday orders; most-ordered item |
| Moving average (3-week weighted) | 0.5×last week + 0.3×prior + 0.2×before | Demand forecast for prep and buying |
| Standard deviation and coefficient of variation | Spread of daily demand | Safety stock sizing; which items need buffers |
| Trend % | (This period − Last period) ÷ Last period × 100 | Weekly growth tracking |
| Pareto / ABC | Rank by revenue or contribution | ~20% of items usually drive ~80% of sales — protect the A-list |
| Seasonality index | Period value ÷ Average period value | Ramadan, winter, payday cycles, Fridays |
| Correlation | Association between two series | Discount depth vs order volume; weather vs soup sales — correlation is not causation, so test before acting |
| Break-even sensitivity | What-if tables on price, cost, volume | Stress-testing the P&L before decisions |

## 12. Inventory Management for Food Delivery

Delivery businesses live on tighter inventory than dine-in: portion drift, packaging counts, and rush-hour speed all leak money invisibly.

### 12.1 Count Cadence

| What | When | Method |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High-value proteins, cheese, oil | Daily close | Weigh-and-log sheet |
| Full perishables | Weekly | Zone-by-zone count, FIFO rotation |
| Dry stores and spices | Monthly | Full stocktake vs book |
| Packaging consumables | Weekly | Count per 100 orders usage rate |

### 12.2 Par Levels and Reorder Points

- Reorder point = (Average daily usage × Lead time days) + Safety stock
- Par level = Usage per replenishment cycle + Safety stock
- Safety stock grows with demand variability (standard deviation) and supplier distance

**Example:** 20 kg chicken used daily, wholesaler delivers next morning (1-day lead), safety stock 8 kg → reorder point = 28 kg. When the stock tag hits 28 kg, order — no feelings, just the number.

### 12.3 Packaging Bill of Materials (per order — count like ingredients)

| Item | Per Order | For 100 Orders |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Food box / wrapper | 1 | 100 |
| Paper bag or carry bag | 1 | 100 |
| Napkins | 2 | 200 |
| Cutlery set (on request only) | 0 – 1 | ≤ 60 — cutlery on request saves money and waste |
| Sauce cups | 1 – 2 | 150 |
| Sticker / seal | 1 | 100 |

Packaging is a real ingredient of delivery food cost — it belongs in the unit economics equation, priced per unit and tracked per 100 orders.

### 12.4 Storage Rules

| Zone | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Chiller (0 – 4 °C) | Raw proteins bottom shelf, ready-to-eat top — never reversed |
| Freezer (−18 °C) | Label with date on entry; use within quality window |
| Dry store | FIFO by default; FEFO (first expiry, first out) for anything dated |
| Danger zone (5 – 60 °C) | Minimize time — prep fast, chill fast, reheat once |
| Delivery staging | Insulated bags pre-heated/pre-cooled; orders out within minutes of readiness |

### 12.5 Variance Discipline

- Variance % = (Book − Physical) ÷ Book × 100 — investigate anything above 2%
- Persistent variance on one item = portion drift or leakage: retrain, re-spec, or relock portions with tools
- Log every adjustment — a stocktake that always "comes right" is a stocktake nobody does honestly

## 13. Waste and Cost Control System

### 13.1 Seven-Day Waste Audit (the starter move)

1. Place a waste sheet by every bin — prep, line, and wash areas
2. Log every discard: item, amount, reason, approximate value
3. Total daily; classify at week end
4. Attack the top 2 reasons only — the Pareto of waste
5. Re-run monthly

**Waste log fields (Template T9):** date, item, quantity, value, reason (spoilage, over-prep, trim excess, cooking error, portion rejection, delivery error), action taken.

### 13.2 Waste Sources and Countermeasures

| Source | Countermeasure |
| --- | --- |
| Over-prepping | Prep par by daypart from moving-average forecast |
| Poor trim technique | Yield training + cutting specs + yield tests |
| Portion drift | Scales, scoops, ladles, patty molds — visual specs fail, tools do not |
| Spoilage | FIFO/FEFO labels, buy-to-par, weekly counts |
| Surplus trim and bones | Convert to stock, sauce bases, staff meals policy with limits |
| Cooking errors | Batch cards with checkpoints; taste-test every batch |
| Delivery errors | Verification checklist before handoff; re-check platform modifiers |

### 13.3 Surplus Rescue Moves

- Today's over-prepped protein → tomorrow's limited-time special at contribution-positive price

## 17. Guardrails and Quality Rules

1. **Never invent prices.** Every price is dated, located, and sourced; assumptions are labeled and must be replaced with real market data.
2. **City and currency first.** No costing begins until the country, city, and currency are confirmed.
3. **Show the math.** Every result is traceable step by step through the formulas in this skill.
4. **Verify platform terms.** Commissions, fees, and payout cycles come from the user's contract — published ranges are only starting points.
5. **Quality is a hard constraint.** Cost reduction never silently degrades spec — changes to portions or ingredients are stated openly and priced.
6. **Food safety is non-negotiable.** Temperatures, storage zones, and allergen flags are flagged in every recipe card.
7. **Respect the user's data.** Sales figures and supplier prices shared in session stay in session.
8. **Mirror the language.** English or বাংলা, matching the user automatically.
9. **Be decisive but honest about uncertainty.** Give a recommendation, then show the confidence and what would change it.
10. **End every answer with a next-step menu** — the session stays interactive.
